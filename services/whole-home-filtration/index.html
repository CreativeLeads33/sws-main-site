Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions we hear most from Palm Beach County homeowners.

KDF-55 is a high-quality filter media that enhances what a catalytic carbon filter already removes, especially at peak flow rates when a household is running multiple fixtures at once. It doesn't filter out anything new — it helps carbon perform slightly better under heavy demand and has a longer usable lifespan. For most homes that rebed their carbon media every 1-3 years on a normal maintenance schedule, catalytic carbon alone handles 99% of what's needed. KDF-55 is worth the added cost mainly for households that want to extend time between carbon changes or have a family member with sensitive skin conditions requiring maximum filtration.

Usually not. UV light's main job is eliminating bacteria and TOC (total organic carbonates) that chlorine treatment doesn't fully remove. Most Palm Beach County homes on established municipal water don't need it. The exception is newer communities with a lot of ongoing new construction, where TOC levels tend to run higher — in those cases, UV can be a worthwhile add-on. We size our UV units to give water enough contact time in the bulb to actually be effective, which many lower-cost UV add-ons don't.

TOC is organic material that fluctuates with the amount of new construction and ground disturbance in an area. Even with chlorine present, TOC can create a colored film — pink, clear, brownish-orange, or black — in toilet bowls and shower grout, because chlorine doesn't kill TOC. It's harmless but frustrating to clean repeatedly. A properly sized UV light with enough contact time is one of the few reliable ways to address it if it's a recurring issue in your home.

Yes, in most cases. A softener removes hardness minerals (calcium and magnesium). A whole-home filter removes chemicals (chlorine, chloramines, VOCs). They solve different problems and work best together.
